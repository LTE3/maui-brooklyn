Dispatches from the Atlas

FOLIO · 02 / 08

Maui Brooklyn began as a postcard never mailed — a love letter between a Kingston grandmother and a Lahaina line cook, folded between pages of a borrowed atlas. We took the corners of those continents, pressed them flat, and opened a tiki kitchen on Myrtle Avenue where the rum still tastes of saltwater and the pineapple is grilled in plain sight.

Here, every cocktail is a cancellation mark on the day. Bartenders work behind a counter of carved teak; servers arrive with menus stitched like passports. The room smells of toasted coconut and cigar leaf. Strangers leave as collectors.

Our kitchen runs on the cross-trade winds of the Caribbean and the Pacific — jerk-spiced shrimp meets shoyu ahi, plantains share a plate with kalua pork, lilikoi shaved ice cools the back of a scotch bonnet. We pour rums from Martinique, Maui, Barbados, and a cellar near the Williamsburg Bridge.

You are invited to thumb through the bar like a stamp book, to find the island you'd most like to be from this evening — and to leave a small ink mark of your own.
