The Premise

Two coastlines, one bar. Tiki rebuilt as geometry.

Maui Brooklyn is a tropical kitchen on Myrtle Avenue where the Caribbean and Hawaii meet at a single bar rail. We pull from jerk smoke and shoyu glaze, from agricole rum and pineapple shrub — then plate it on a strict white grid. No grass-skirt clichés. The mask is a circle, the palm is a triangle, the cocktail is a color study.

The room is small, the volume runs hot, and the rum list runs long. Painkillers come dusted with nutmeg, mai tais arrive over crushed ice, the kitchen sends out fire shrimp and short rib loco moco until close. Built for the after-work shift, the late-night detour, and the friend you brought from out of town who didn't believe Brooklyn could feel like Lahaina.

03 · The Room

Hot lamps. Cold rum. Loud bass.

Bamboo trim against a black wall, a long rail of vintage tiki mugs, and a sound system that leans Studio One on Tuesday and Honolulu surf on Saturday. The booths are deep, the candles are real, and the kitchen sends plates until the last guest leaves.

Bring a date. Bring six friends. Bring the colleague who only drinks gin — we have something for them too. The patio opens May through October.
