Feature The House of Maui Brooklyn — An Editorial § 02 / VIII

An Island, Two Avenues Off the Line.

Notes on a kitchen that translates between two warm seas — and the corner of Myrtle that received it without question.

Brooklyn — The first thing the room does is forgive you for the F train. The second thing it does is hand you something cold in a vessel shaped like a face. Maui Brooklyn opened on a Tuesday and behaves, eight months later, like a place that has always been here — a stubborn, sun-coloured holdout against the moods of February.

Co-owners — a Trinidadian pastry cook and a Honolulu-trained bartender — built the kitchen as a long conversation between the two oceans they grew up loving. Jerk pork shoulder shares a plate with kalua-style cabbage. Curry goat appears beside lomi-lomi salmon. The drinks are exact and not precious: orgeat made on a Monday, lime squeezed to order, pineapple cored by hand.

We are a Caribbean kitchen with a Hawaiian conscience, and a corner of Myrtle Avenue with a permanent low tide.

What carries the room, though, is not concept but climate. The lighting flatters. The music — surf guitar, ska, a little Don Tiki — leans the right amount of dumb. Bamboo on the ceiling, tin overhead fan, a single shrine to Pele behind the well. On a Saturday at ten the bar is six deep and somehow still kind.

You leave smelling like coconut sunscreen, which on a Brooklyn winter night is the most generous trick a restaurant can play. The Times recommends arriving early, ordering the painkiller, and not asking what's in the dark rum until the second one.

Lifestyle The Room, After Dark — A Visual Dispatch § 04 / VIII

A Lanai on Myrtle.

On the architecture of warmth: bamboo, brass, candlelight, and a tin-roof fan kept just slow enough.

The Look

Bamboo ceiling slats, a tin fan that turns at exactly the speed of a slow conversation, and brass that has been allowed to remember its tarnish.

The room is not a theme park. It is a house — built corner by corner around the question of whether a Brooklyn winter could be politely ignored. The owners answered yes. The answer holds.

The Sound

A surf guitar trio plays Friday and Saturday. The rest of the week, vinyl: Don Tiki, Arthur Lyman, Toots, the occasional ska 7-inch when the night calls for it.

By midnight on a Saturday the bar is six deep and somehow the bartenders still know your face. The trick, they say, is sleep, lime, and a clean shaker. We are inclined to believe them.
