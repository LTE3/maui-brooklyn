Chapter One — The Setup

Two oceans, one back room.

Maui Brooklyn is what happens when a Port-au-Prince kitchen and a Honolulu beach bar walk into a Bushwick storefront and refuse to leave. We slow-cook the Caribbean — jerk, escovitch, sofrito, scotch bonnet — and pour it shoulder-to-shoulder with the long, syrupy ghosts of Don the Beachcomber and Trader Vic. The result reads like a paperback you couldn't put down: salt, smoke, citrus, and rum dark enough to keep secrets.

Step in off Myrtle Avenue and the air changes — bamboo, cane sugar, a fan turning slow overhead. Our cocktails are built one at a time, ingredients hand-cut, ice carved, no shortcuts. The kitchen runs late. The records run later. Bring a friend, an alibi, or both. We open the door at five and we don't ask questions.

Chapter Three — The Atmosphere

A dim room with intent.

Low light. Wicker. A fan you can hear. Records you can feel. We built Maui Brooklyn for the after-shift crowd and the off-the-clock romantics — the ones who treat a Tuesday like a holiday and a Saturday like a misdemeanor.

“Walked in for a Mai Tai. Walked out three hours later, vaguely engaged.”
